## Company context

Nimbus is a B2B productivity app that helps small teams manage projects, tasks, and client communication. Customers can start with a 14-day trial and upgrade to Starter, Pro, or Business plans.

The main growth concern is that trial signups are increasing, but trial-to-paid conversion has remained flat for the last 90 days.

## Data explored

I reviewed the five core tables:

- `users`
- `organizations`
- `subscriptions`
- `user_events`
- `support_tickets`

Initial observations:

- `users` contains both customer accounts and internal/test accounts.
- Internal and test accounts can be identified using `is_internal`, `@nimbus.io` email addresses, and `created_by = 'qa_seed'`.
- `subscriptions` contains trial, active, and cancelled records.
- `user_events` contains product-activity events, including possible duplicates.
- `support_tickets` includes resolved, open, awaiting-customer, and escalated tickets.

## Data-quality observations

1. Internal and test accounts are stored in the same tables as customer accounts. These accounts must be excluded from customer-facing business metrics.

2. `user_events` may contain duplicate records caused by page refreshes, network retries, or ingestion issues. `event_id` appears to be the preferred deduplication key.

3. Some trial records appear to remain in `trial` status beyond the expected 14-day trial period. This raises a question about whether trial expiration is recorded or whether expired trials remain in the trial population.

4. Some active paid users have no recorded events. This may be because the billing owner is not the primary product user, or because event activity is generated by other members of the same organization.

5. Support tickets include unresolved, escalated, and awaiting-customer cases. A closed ticket does not necessarily mean the customer’s issue was successfully resolved.

## Metric I would track more carefully

**Metric:** Trial-to-paid conversion rate.

**Why it matters:** This is the central metric behind the current growth concern. If the trial population includes expired trials that were never updated, the conversion rate will be understated.

**How I would define it:**

- Population: non-internal, non-test users who started a trial.
- Conversion: a trial with a `converted_at` timestamp.
- Observation window: conversion within 30 days of `trial_started_at`, subject to confirmation from product and engineering.
- Exclusions: internal users, test accounts, duplicate subscription records, and trials without a completed observation window.

## Initial hypothesis

Customers who do not complete onboarding may be less likely to convert, but this is only a hypothesis. It needs to be validated against actual conversion, event, and support-ticket data before making a recommendation.
